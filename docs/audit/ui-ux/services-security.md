# UI/UX Audit — Security Services (`/services/security`)

## Summary
This is the weakest of the 5 detail pages purely due to a missing hero image, which produces both a visual quality gap and a content-accuracy problem (the alt text describes a photo that doesn't exist). The service-card content itself is on par with Mechanical and reasonably solid, but the unresolved PSIRA registration placeholder undermines the compliance trust-signal this page is explicitly trying to build. Also affected by shared layout issues — see `services-layout-shared.md`.

## Findings
| Severity | Area | Finding | Recommendation | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Image / alt text mismatch | `image='/images/placeholder.svg'` — a generic dark gradient-and-grid graphic — is used as the full-bleed hero background, but `imageAlt='Phehlwana Group security officer at a commercial building entrance'` describes a specific photograph that does not exist. This is a real accessibility/content-integrity issue: screen reader users are told they're looking at a photo of a security officer when they're actually shown an abstract gradient. It's also the only detail page (of 5) without real photography, so it stands out visually against the other 4 pages and against the overview grid. | Source or commission a real security-services photo (matching the treatment of the other 4 hero images) and update `imageAlt` to match, or if no photo is available yet, use accurate alt text describing the placeholder rather than a fabricated scene. | `src/pages/services/security.astro:38-39` |
| High | Unresolved placeholder copy shipped to production | The compliance note reads "PSIRA registration number **to be confirmed by client**" — this is internal/agency placeholder language that has leaked into live customer-facing copy. For a security company, an unconfirmed PSIRA registration number directly undermines the credibility of the compliance claim being made in the same sentence. | Replace with the actual PSIRA registration number before this page is considered launch-ready, or omit the specific-number claim entirely until available (state general compliance without implying a number is pending). | `src/pages/services/security.astro:78-83` |
| Medium | Cascading overview-page impact | Because this page's `image` prop is the placeholder, and `services/index.astro`'s `serviceImages` map also independently points `Security` at the same `/images/placeholder.svg` (`src/pages/services/index.astro:16`), the placeholder gap is visible in *two* places (overview card grid + this page's hero) rather than being contained to one. | Fixing the image asset once resolves both locations — flagging the compounding effect since it doubles the visible impact of a single missing asset. | `src/pages/services/security.astro:38`; `src/pages/services/index.astro:16` |

## What's Working Well
- The 6 service cards (Manned Guarding through Site Security) are well-chosen and cover the expected breadth of a security offering without padding.
- Correct, specific regulatory reference (PSIRA, Act 56 of 2001) embedded in both a card body (Risk Assessment) and the dedicated compliance callout — the intent behind the callout (build trust via regulation) is sound even though execution is currently undermined by the placeholder registration number (see High finding above).
- Compliance callout uses a distinct shield icon and `bg-accent/40` treatment, visually differentiating it from the plain service cards above — a reasonable pattern that could be reused on other pages needing a similar regulatory callout (e.g. Cleaning's NEM:WA note, which currently uses a plainer style).
- Correct `text-primary` usage throughout for decorative bullets and the shield icon; no interactive-color misuse.
